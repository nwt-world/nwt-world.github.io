## Overview

This route crosses the remote Southwest Altiplano from San Pedro de Atacama to Uyuni.

The Salar de Uyuni is the final highlight, but the first two days are equally important:
colored lagoons, volcano views, geothermal fields, thermal pools, rock formations, flamingos, and almost empty high-altitude desert.

The route is tour-based by design.
There is almost no public transport between the natural sites, roads are mostly unpaved, fuel and repair facilities are extremely limited, and accommodation is concentrated in a few remote settlements.
A shared or private 4×4 tour is the practical way to travel through this region.

This is better suited to experienced travelers who are comfortable with:  
• long 4×4 driving days  
• basic accommodation  
• very cold nights  
• limited facilities  
• rapid travel above 4,000 m  

Before starting from Chile, spend several nights acclimatizing in <a href="/itinerary/chile-atacama">San Pedro de Atacama</a>.

**Region:** Southwest Bolivia / Potosí  
**Overall time:** 3 days one-way San Pedro → Uyuni; 4 days if returning to San Pedro  
**Main focus:** volcanoes, colored lagoons, Altiplano desert, wildlife, Salar de Uyuni  
**Best overall season:** September–November for dry roads and less severe cold than midwinter

## Tour format and accommodation

Standard shared tours:
- Usually use Toyota Land Cruiser or similar 4×4 vehicles
- Common configuration is up to 6 passengers plus the driver
- Meals and overnight accommodation are normally included
- Park entrances, thermal pools, and some smaller fees may be paid separately in cash

Accommodation:
- The first night around Villamar is usually the most basic
- Standard rooms may have no room heating even when outside temperatures fall below freezing
- Hot water, showers, electricity, and charging can be limited or available only at specific times
- The second night near the Salar is often somewhat better, especially in salt hostels

The landscapes are the reason to take this route; accommodation should be treated as functional overnight shelter.

## Starting from La Paz instead

Uyuni can also be reached from La Paz.

La Paz → Uyuni:
- Direct BoA flight: about 1 hour
- Overnight bus: roughly 9–10 hours

Current direct flights from Uyuni operate to La Paz, making this the simplest air connection for continuing through Bolivia.

Altitude:
- Central La Paz: ~3,650 m
- El Alto airport: ~4,060 m

Tour choice matters:
- A 1-day Uyuni tour mainly covers the Salar, Colchani, and nearby sights
- A 3-day tour from Uyuni includes the southern Altiplano, colored lagoons, deserts, volcano viewpoints, and geothermal areas
- The 3-day route can finish back in Uyuni or continue across the border to San Pedro de Atacama

For this itinerary, San Pedro → Uyuni is preferable because the route naturally starts with the most remote southern landscapes and finishes with the Salar.

---

## Continuing from Uyuni

Uyuni has useful onward connections, but it is not a major transport hub.

La Paz:
- Direct BoA flight
- Direct overnight buses

Potosí:
- Regular buses
- Useful if continuing through southern / central Bolivia

Sucre:
- Direct overnight buses operate
- Another option is Uyuni → Potosí → Sucre

Tupiza / Villazón:
- Bus connections are available
- Useful if continuing south toward Argentina

There are currently no useful direct international flights from Uyuni; La Paz is the main air connection.

## Best season

September–November:
- Best overall balance for this full route
- Mostly dry roads
- Good access across the Salar
- Less severe cold than June–August
- Nights at Villamar can still fall below freezing

December–March:
- Warmer than winter
- Best chance of the Salar mirror effect, especially January–February
- Rain can change the route
- Isla Incahuasi and some Salar crossings may become inaccessible

May–August:
- Driest and most predictable road conditions
- Clear skies
- Full dry-salt-flat landscapes
- Coldest period, especially at the first-night accommodation and before sunrise

There is no single best season:
choose September–November for the most balanced expedition, or the rainy season if the mirror effect is the priority.

## What to bring

Bring:
- Bolivianos in cash for park entrances and small local payments
- Very warm layers, including gloves and hat
- Small day bag for items needed inside the 4×4 (large luggage normally travels on the roof of the 4×4 under a protective cover)
- Water, sunscreen, sunglasses, and lip protection
- Swimsuit and towel if using the thermal pools
