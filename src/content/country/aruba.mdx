## 🌍 Why Choose Aruba?

Aruba is primarily a destination for easy beach holidays. Its greatest advantage is the combination of wide white-sand beaches, calm swimming conditions on the west coast, dependable sunshine, and convenient services.

It is less about extensive sightseeing than spending several comfortable days by the sea, with a little snorkeling, island nature, and local culture between beach visits.

General destination information: <a href="https://www.aruba.com/us/">Aruba Tourism</a>.

---

## 🏖️ Beaches: Access and Facilities

Aruba's natural beaches are public and free to enter, including those in front of resorts. Beach chairs, umbrellas, watersports, and private-island day passes are separate paid services. Facilities vary considerably.

| Beach | Facilities and character |
|---|---|
| **Eagle Beach** | Wide, beautiful white sand; comparatively quiet, with limited services on some stretches, occasional shade, and loungers available near hotels |
| **Palm Beach** | Calm water and a long resort strip; plentiful restaurants, bars, watersports, and rentable loungers; busiest atmosphere |
| **Arashi Beach** | Sheltered northern beach with palapas, parking, a beach bar, and chair rental; reachable by bus |
| **Boca Catalina** | Small snorkeling cove with some shaded huts and little commercial infrastructure; bring water and supplies |
| **Baby Beach** | Shallow southern bay with food options and rented chairs; more distant from the main hotel area |
| **Mangel Halto** | Mangroves, rocky/sandy entries, and snorkeling; limited services, better for water activities than a classic beach day |
| **Surfside Beach** | Modest, convenient beach near Oranjestad with nearby food and some facilities; useful without traveling to the hotel strip |

The exposed north and east coasts are very different: dramatic scenery, but generally stronger surf and less suitable swimming conditions.

### 🐠 Snorkeling and Boat Trips

- **Boca Catalina / Malmok** — accessible shore snorkeling over rocks and reef areas, with little beach infrastructure at Malmok.
- **Arashi Beach** — sandy swimming and nearby snorkeling in one place.
- **Mangel Halto** — reefs and mangroves with more interesting underwater terrain; suitable for confident swimmers in appropriate conditions.
- **Antilla shipwreck** — one of Aruba's best-known snorkeling/diving sights, normally visited on an organized boat trip rather than directly from shore.

Beach snorkeling is a useful part of an Aruba holiday, but the island's defining attraction remains its wide sandy beaches.

### 🌵 Arikok and Other Landscapes

- **Arikok National Park** — cactus-covered hills, dry valleys, coastal scenery, and limestone caves. Entry requires a conservation pass (currently US$22 per adult). Check <a href="https://www.acf.aw/experiences">Aruba Conservation Foundation</a> for park hours, fees, and ranger-led walks.
- **Fontein and Quadirikiri Caves** — cave formations and historical/archaeological features within the park; verify access and opening hours before visiting.
- **Natural Pool (Conchi)** — striking volcanic-rock pool on a rough-access route. A suitable 4x4 or guided excursion is the practical option; an ordinary rental car should not be assumed sufficient.
- **California Lighthouse** — a short scenic stop at the northern end of the island.

For island sightseeing, snorkeling cruises, or guided off-road transport, compare <a href="https://depalm.com/">De Palm Tours</a> and <a href="https://abc-aruba.com/">ABC Tours Aruba</a>. Tours vary considerably in which areas they actually visit.

### 🏛️ Towns and Culture

- **Oranjestad** — compact capital with Dutch-Caribbean architecture, waterfront streets, shops, and restaurants.
- **San Nicolas** — colorful street murals and a more local cultural stop, suitable to combine with the southern beaches.

## 🏨 Where to Stay

| Base | Main advantage | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Eagle Beach / low-rise area** | Wide beach, quieter surroundings, bus stops along the coast | Restaurants and supermarkets may require a walk or short bus ride |
| **Palm Beach** | Beach, restaurants, shops, and tourist services within walking distance | More crowded, commercial, and often expensive |
| **Oranjestad** | City services, potentially cheaper rooms, main Arubus terminal | Best long beaches require a bus ride |
| **Noord / inland apartments** | Self-catering options and sometimes better accommodation value | Check walking distance carefully; many addresses are easier with a car |
| **Savaneta / southern coast** | Quiet local setting and access to the southern part of the island | Fewer services and limited convenience for hotel-strip beaches |

For budgeting, simple inland or city apartments can sometimes be around US$80–150 per night; beach-area apartments often run roughly US$130–250, while beachfront hotels and resorts may be US$250–500+ in busy periods. These are broad planning ranges, not live rates.

With public buses, a property near the western coastal route is particularly convenient. Check the actual bus stop, supermarket, and beach walking distance rather than relying only on the neighborhood name.

## 🚌 Getting Around

- **Public buses:** <a href="https://arubus.com/routes-schedules-current/">Arubus routes and schedules</a> are useful for most classic beach trips. Route **L10** connects Oranjestad with Eagle Beach, Palm Beach, and Arashi. Route **L2** serves Oranjestad, the airport road, and San Nicolas; **L900** connects San Nicolas with Baby Beach. The southern trip normally takes more time and a transfer. Check the last bus before leaving.
- **Bus fares:** A standard ride is currently US$2.60 / AWG 4.50; return and day tickets are available. See <a href="https://arubus.com/fares-passes/">official fares and passes</a>.
- **Rental car:** offers flexibility for Arikok, dispersed southern locations, and beach hopping. Regular cars suit main paved roads; rough national-park tracks require an appropriate vehicle or an excursion.
- **Taxis:** regulated fares, useful for short transfers or evenings but costly as a substitute for daily beach buses. Calculate routes on <a href="https://www.taxi.aw/">Taxi Aruba</a>.
- **Excursions:** a practical alternative for nature sites not served by Arubus, especially Arikok and the Natural Pool.

## ✈️ Airport and Connections

Queen Beatrix International Airport (AUA) is near Oranjestad.

- **Taxi:** readily available at arrivals, with government-regulated fares. Check the <a href="https://www.airportaruba.com/taxi-transportation">airport taxi information</a> and fare calculator before your trip.
- **Transfer or rental car:** bookable in advance, useful for beachfront accommodation or a late arrival.
- **Public bus:** Arubus **L2** stops on the road outside airport property, not directly at the terminal doors. Note that Arubus accepts carry-on items but not larger luggage, so this option is suitable only when traveling light. See <a href="https://www.airportaruba.com/index.php/public-transportation">airport bus information</a>.

Aruba connects to <a href="/country/curacao/">Curaçao</a> by short flights, including <a href="https://www.flydivi.com/">Divi Divi Air</a>; there is no regular passenger ferry. Flights via <a href="/country/panama/">Panama</a> are another option, including connections through <a href="https://www.copaair.com/">Copa Airlines</a>.

**Important — entry requirement:** Complete Aruba's <a href="https://edcardaruba.aw/">official Online ED Card</a> within the 7 days before departure. A US$20 sustainability fee applies to most adult air visitors, with exemptions; payment is part of the official process. Avoid third-party ED Card websites.

## 🌤️ Seasons and Practical Details

- **Weather:** hot and often sunny throughout the year, with low direct hurricane exposure. December–April is generally the driest and busiest period; October–December can bring more showers without usually becoming a full-day rain destination.
- **Costs:** public beach access and buses are inexpensive, but accommodation, restaurant meals, and organized excursions drive up the total budget. Apartments with kitchens can reduce food costs.
- **Currency:** Aruban florin (AWG); US dollars and cards are commonly accepted in tourist areas. Keep some local cash for small purchases or buses.
- **Safety:** generally comfortable by Caribbean standards, with routine precautions for belongings at beaches and in crowded areas.
